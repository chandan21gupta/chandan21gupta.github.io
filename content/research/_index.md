I’m interested in **AI for Science**: how machine learning can help researchers form hypotheses, design experiments, interpret evidence, and make progress in systems where answers are uncertain or costly to obtain. My work has moved across several scientific domains, and each has shaped how I think about the promise and limits of AI in research.

### A direction: scientific agents

At IIT Delhi, I work on self-improving agents for scientific problems, especially settings where outcomes cannot be checked with a simple, automatic reward. One strand of this work is material synthesis: developing agents that can improve their own harnesses and propose experimental protocols for a target material.

I also co-developed **Corral**, an open-source framework for evaluating language-model agents across eight scientific domains and more than 25,000 runs. The evaluation examined tasks ranging from executing workflows to forming hypotheses. It surfaced a gap between reaching an answer and reasoning reliably: agents often failed to account for evidence that contradicted their conclusions. This work was presented orally at the **NeurIPS 2025 AI for Science Workshop**; the full paper is under review. [Read the paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=OLJp4ndI7i).

### Scientific domains I’ve explored

- **Materials science:** Self-improving agents for proposing experimental synthesis protocols.
- **Computational biology:** Inferring human aging trajectories from cross-sectional DNA methylation data, without longitudinal measurements. [MLCB paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06583).
- **Physics:** Learning physical dynamics and generalized Hamiltonians with symplectic neural methods. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27029).
- **Agriculture:** Earlier applied work on crop disease identification, yield estimation, and decision support at Wadhwani AI.

These projects differ in their data, tools, and standards of evidence. Together, they motivate my interest in AI systems that can work with scientific methods and domain knowledge, rather than treating scientific discovery as prediction alone.
